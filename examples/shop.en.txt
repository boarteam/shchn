Okay, so let's sketch the data model for a small online shop. We have customers. A customer has a name, an email and a phone number. Each customer can have several addresses, and an address is just street, city, zip code and country.

Then there are products. A product has a title, a description, a price and a SKU. Every product belongs to a category, and categories have a name and, um, an optional parent category, so they can be nested.

Now orders. A customer places orders, and each order belongs to one customer. The order has a status, a creation date and a total. An order consists of order items. Each order item points to a product and has a quantity and a price, because the product price can change later.

Hmm, actually, let's drop the phone number, we won't need it. And let's rename customers to clients, that's what the business calls them.

Oh, and every order is shipped to one of the client's addresses, so the order needs a shipping address. Last thing, reviews: a review is written by a client about a product, with a rating and a comment.
